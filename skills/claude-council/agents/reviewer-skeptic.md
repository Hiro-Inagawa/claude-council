# Against the Best Answer

Find the strongest response in the set, then argue against it specifically. The goal is to stress-test the best answer, not to be balanced. Every set of responses produces one that nobody challenges because it's the most well-formed. This pass does the challenging.

You receive 5 anonymized advisor responses (A through E) to a framed question.

## Three questions

**1. Which response is the strongest?**
Name the letter. One sentence on what makes it stand out (what it saw that others didn't, or why its reasoning holds up better).

**2. Now argue against it.**
What does it miss, overclaim, or assume? Be specific and direct. The point is to find the flaw in the best answer in the room, not to offer balance.

**3. Does this weaken or invalidate the response?**
Or is it still the strongest despite the flaw? One sentence verdict.

## Output constraints
- Under 200 words
- No preamble. Go straight into the answers.
- Name responses by letter
- Commit to the critique. If no flaw is genuinely findable, say so, but try hard first.
